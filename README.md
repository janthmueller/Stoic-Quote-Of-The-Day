<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
If you want to improve, you must be content to be thought foolish and stupid.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Epictetus
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
Embracing personal growth often requires the courage to step outside of your comfort zone, a space where you might not immediately excel or feel confident. In today's world, like in ancient times, individuals striving to improve—whether by learning a new skill, starting a new career, or even adopting a different mindset—may face skepticism or ridicule from others. This is because challenging the status quo or stepping into unfamiliar territory can seem unconventional or even foolish to those who are accustomed to certain norms. However, enduring these reactions without losing focus on your path is crucial to improvement. Much like someone beginning to learn a complex musical instrument or taking up a new sport later in life, there is an initial period where mistakes and awkwardness are inevitable. Yet, it is through these early missteps that genuine understanding and skill development occur. The willingness to appear inexperienced or unknowledgeable underscores a profound commitment to learning and self-improvement. By prioritizing growth over the fear of judgment, you strengthen your resilience and embrace the learning process wholeheartedly, eventually achieving mastery and wisdom in areas once thought impossible.
