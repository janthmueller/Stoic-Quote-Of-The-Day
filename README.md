<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
You can put up with a change of place if only the place is changed.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Seneca
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
Embracing change becomes much more manageable when we shift our perspective rather than solely focusing on the external circumstances. Seneca suggests that while it's natural to feel unsettled by a change of location—whether that means moving to a new city for a job, relocating due to family needs, or simply adjusting to new environments—what truly eases the transition is adjusting our mindset. In our fast-paced world, jobs and life opportunities often require us to uproot ourselves, sometimes leaving behind comfort and familiarity. But if we view the move not as a loss but as a chance for growth, new experiences, and a broader outlook, the anxiety that accompanies change can diminish. By cultivating an attitude that welcomes new beginnings with curiosity and openness, we allow ourselves to adapt more smoothly. Each place offers unique lessons and opportunities, and by focusing on the gains rather than what we leave behind, we can find contentment regardless of where life takes us. This approach encourages us to find inner stability, ensuring that our well-being is not dependent on external coordinates but rooted in our own flexibility and resilience.
