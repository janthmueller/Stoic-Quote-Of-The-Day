<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
Never value anything as profitable that compels you to break your promise, to lose your self-respect, to hate any man, to suspect, to curse, to act the hypocrite, to desire anything that needs walls and curtains.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Marcus Aurelius
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
In life, the notion of profit or gain often tempts us to compromise on our values, but true wisdom lies in recognizing that nothing worthy comes at the cost of integrity. It invites us to examine the deeper consequences of our actions, emphasizing that real profit is not financial or material but tied to character and virtue. Imagine being offered a promotion that requires undermining a colleague, breaching their trust, or behaving unethically to gain favor with your superior. Such a situation might promise immediate benefits, but it erodes your self-respect and sows discord within your soul. Stoicism guides you to prioritize what nurtures your character over transient pleasures or status. It's about refusing opportunities that lead you to harbor resentment, engage in deceit, or rely on secrecy. Think of a scenario where a friendship could sour into resentment over money or success, urging you to remember that lasting fulfillment is more connected to keeping faith and fostering sincere relationships. Ultimately, this teaching reminds us that genuine contentment and the respect we hold for ourselves are far more enriching than any superficial gain that demands the sacrifice of our core principles.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
