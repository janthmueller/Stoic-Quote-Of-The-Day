    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
Errare humanum est, sed perseverare diabolicum: 'to err is human, but to persist (in the mistake) is diabolical.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Seneca
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
Embracing our humanity means recognizing that making mistakes is an inevitable part of life. Seneca's insight urges us to acknowledge our errors with humility and view them as opportunities for learning and growth. It's perfectly natural to stumble and falter, whether in personal relationships, work environments, or while pursuing our passions. However, the potential harm arises when we persistently repeat these mistakes without reflection or willingness to change. In modern terms, this could be likened to repeatedly ignoring constructive feedback at work, or continuously making unhealthy choices despite knowing their consequences. Such persistence without self-awareness can lead to destructive patterns that not only impact us but also those around us. The "diabolical" aspect serves as a decorative warning against allowing our pride or stubbornness to override our ability to adapt and improve. Instead of clinging to our faults, we should seek to understand the reasons behind them, applying that knowledge to make more thoughtful and deliberate choices moving forward. By doing so, we align ourselves more closely with a path of wisdom and personal development, reducing unnecessary suffering and opening doors to more harmonious experiences.
