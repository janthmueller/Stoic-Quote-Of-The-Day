    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
Associate with those who will make a better man of you. Welcome those whom you yourself can improve. The process is mutual; for men learn while they teach.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Seneca
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
Seneca's words emphasize the importance of surrounding ourselves with people who inspire growth and enable us to be our best selves. In today’s world, this can mean choosing friends and mentors who challenge our perspectives, encourage our ambitions, and align with the values we aspire to embody. It also entails being open to helping others grow, enhancing our own understanding in the process. For instance, in a work setting, collaborating with a colleague who possesses a skill you admire can be beneficial not only because you learn from them but also because teaching them about your areas of expertise reinforces your knowledge. This dynamic creates a cycle of mutual improvement; it reminds us that personal development is not a solitary journey, but a communal one where we grow by learning and teaching together. Engaging both with those who elevate us and those whom we can uplift ensures a balanced, continuous path to self-betterment.
