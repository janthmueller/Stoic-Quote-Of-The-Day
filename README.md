<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
Choose not to be harmed—and you won’t feel harmed. Don’t feel harmed—and you haven’t been.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Marcus Aurelius
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
The essence of Marcus Aurelius' insight encourages a mindset where one's inner peace is not disrupted by external events or the actions of others. In today's world, where criticism and adversity are commonplace, either in-person or through digital interactions, this Stoic principle is a guide toward emotional resilience. Imagine receiving negative feedback on social media; it can feel like an affront to your self-esteem. Yet, by choosing to interpret these words as mere opinions, rather than personal attacks, you protect your inner tranquility. This requires a conscious effort to distance your self-worth from the volatility of external judgments. By understanding that feeling harmed is a choice, not a necessity, you empower yourself to maintain composure in the face of adversity. In essence, Marcus Aurelius reminds us that true harm occurs only when we allow external events to disturb our inner peace. Through this perspective, we grasp the freedom to remain unaffected, cultivating a mind that remains serene and untroubled amidst life's inevitable challenges.
