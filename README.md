<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
They lose the day in expectation of the night, and the night in fear of the dawn.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Seneca
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
Seneca’s insight touches on the human tendency to become trapped in cycles of anticipation and fear, preventing us from fully embracing the present moment. This wisdom reminds us that by constantly longing for what is to come or fearing what lies ahead, we squander the opportunities and richness of the current moment. Just as someone might wait all day for their evening plans, only to spend those moments worrying about the next day, we often occupy ourselves with what’s next, rather than enjoying what is now. In modern life, this might manifest as spending our workday dreaming of a vacation, then spending that vacation worrying about work. Such preoccupation prevents us from appreciating the peace and possibilities offered by today. Recognizing this habit allows us to pause, liberate ourselves from this futile cycle, and awaken to the beauty and potential of the here and now, finding joy and fulfillment in the small, present moments that we might otherwise overlook.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
