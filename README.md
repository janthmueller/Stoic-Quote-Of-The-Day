<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
It is always our choice whether or not we wish to pay the price for life's rewards. And often it is best for us not to pay the price, for the price might be our integrity.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Epictetus
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
Epictetus reminds us that life constantly presents opportunities, each with a price tag that is not always monetary but often ethical or moral. In our everyday pursuits, such as career advancements, financial gains, or social acceptance, we're frequently faced with decisions about how far we're willing to go to achieve our goals. Perhaps a colleague suggests cutting corners on a project, or an opportunity arises to gain an advantage through dishonest means. Here, the price isn’t merely the effort or resources spent but the compromise on our core values and personal integrity. Epictetus advises that it is crucial to evaluate these situations and consider whether the potential reward is truly worth the erosion of our character. Maintaining integrity often means walking away from what might superficially seem like success, preserving a sense of self-respect and inner peace that ultimately proves more rewarding than any external accolade. By conscientiously choosing to uphold our principles, even when it seems costly, we align ourselves with a more profound, enduring sense of fulfillment.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
