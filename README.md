<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
Ambition means tying your well-being to what other people say or do. Self-indulgence means tying it to the things that happen to you. Sanity means tying it to your own actions.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Marcus Aurelius
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
In today’s world, it's easy to see how our well-being might feel dependent on external factors. If we find ourselves constantly seeking approval or recognition from others—perhaps through social media likes or professional accolades—our happiness becomes tied to ambition, as we chase after what others think. Similarly, if we allow our mood and sense of self-worth to fluctuate solely based on the events happening around us, like winning a promotion or experiencing a setback, we fall into the trap of self-indulgence, our internal state perpetually at the mercy of the external world. Marcus Aurelius's wisdom encourages us to seek sanity by anchoring our well-being in our own actions. This means focusing on what we can control: our own efforts, responses, and choices. Whether it’s through setting a personal goal or handling a difficult situation with grace, when we prioritize our actions and how they align with our values, we cultivate a stable sense of well-being. This approach builds resilience, allowing us to weather life’s ups and downs with a deep-seated peace that does not depend on the whims of others or the unpredictable nature of life’s events.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
