<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
You only have to doze a moment, and all is lost. For ruin and salvation both have their source inside you.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Epictetus
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
In this reflection, Epictetus emphasizes the importance of constant vigilance over our thoughts and actions. It is not external events that dictate our state of being, but rather our internal responses to them. Just as a momentary lapse in attention while driving can lead to an accident, so too can a brief period of inattention in managing our thoughts and emotions lead us astray. Consider how easy it is to let a single moment of anger escalate, potentially harming relationships or personal well-being. Alternatively, cultivating an inner discipline allows us to transform challenges into opportunities for growth. Every choice, each deliberate thought, contributes to our ruin or salvation. This notion invites us to realize that we are the architects of our fate, holding the power to steer our lives toward fulfillment or despair. By staying mindful and attentive to what is within, we harness our inner strength, navigating daily trials with poise and purpose. In a world full of distractions and uncertainties, grounding ourselves in this stoic principle can provide a stable foundation from which to build a resilient and meaningful life.
