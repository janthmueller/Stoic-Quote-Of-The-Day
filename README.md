    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
That day, which you fear as being the end of all things, is the birthday of your eternity.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Seneca
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
The fear of an ending often looms large in our minds—but what if we could see it as a fresh beginning instead? Seneca encourages us to shift our perspective on events we dread, like endings or potentially irreversible changes, suggesting that they might actually mark the start of something enduring and meaningful. Consider graduation day: while it marks the end of a familiar and structured life chapter, it's also the beginning of new opportunities, adventures, and personal growth. Much like stepping through a doorway into an uncharted room, the end of something signals the birth of a vast potential we haven’t yet imagined. In broader life events, from changing careers to moving to a new city, endings can feel daunting, but these transitions can also invite us into a realm of possibilities and insights that shape our journey forward. By embracing this mindset, we transform fear into curiosity and greater acceptance of life's natural flow, continually renewing ourselves in the face of change.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
