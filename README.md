<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
Do not run hither and thither and distract yourself by changing your abode; for such restlessness is the sign of a disordered spirit.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Seneca
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
In today's fast-paced world, it's easy to find ourselves constantly searching for something new, whether it's moving cities, switching jobs, or seeking novel experiences. Seneca's wisdom reminds us that this restlessness often stems from an unsettled mind rather than a genuine need for change. When we're endlessly "running hither and thither," it may be because we're attempting to escape an internal discomfort by changing our external surroundings. This can lead to a cycle where no place or situation feels satisfying because the root cause of our disquiet—our inner state—remains unaddressed. For instance, someone might believe that moving to a bustling city will bring them happiness, yet after settling in, they might discover that the dissatisfaction follows them. Instead of seeking external validation or excitement, we can find tranquility within by reflecting on and resolving our internal conflicts. This internal alignment allows us to find peace and stability regardless of where we are. By focusing on cultivating a healthy inner spirit, we can appreciate our lives and surroundings more fully, without the need for constant upheaval in search of fulfillment.
