<p align="center">
    <em><!--START_SECTION:quote-text-->
Not to be overwhelmed by what you imagine, but just do what you can and should.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-text--></em><br>
    <strong>— <!--START_SECTION:quote-author-->
Marcus Aurelius
<!--END_SECTION:quote-author--></strong>
</p>

<div align="center" style="max-width:600px;margin:0 auto;">
<!--START_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
In the bustling chaos of modern life, it's easy to become engulfed by a tidal wave of worries and imagined scenarios. Marcus Aurelius reminds us to anchor ourselves by focusing on the tangible, actionable steps available to us in the present moment. Instead of allowing our minds to conjure overwhelming possibilities and get trapped in a cycle of anxiety and fear about what might happen, we can break down our challenges into manageable tasks. For instance, consider a student facing exam season with myriad subjects to study. Rather than being paralyzed by fear of potential failure and the enormity of the material, the student can take a calm, proactive approach by setting a daily study schedule and tackling each subject incrementally. Similarly, in a demanding workplace environment, an employee might feel daunted by a seemingly endless to-do list. By prioritizing tasks, focusing on one item at a time, and letting go of unproductive worrying about outcomes beyond immediate control, both student and employee can navigate their responsibilities more effectively and with greater peace of mind. This mindset helps ground us, keeping us productive and centered, as we take meaningful strides towards our goals without being sidetracked by our apprehensions.
<!--END_SECTION:quote-interpretation-->
